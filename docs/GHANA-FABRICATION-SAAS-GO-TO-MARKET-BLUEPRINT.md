# Veloxa Ghana Fabrication SaaS Go-To-Market Blueprint

**Objective:** secure five paying Ghanaian aluminium, glass and façade-fabrication companies before entering other African markets.

**Prepared:** 21 August 2026  
**Commercial premise:** Veloxa owns the platform; each customer subscribes to its configured workspace. Sofaamy is the reference-design partner and early proof point, not the codebase owner.

## 1. The wedge: do not sell “software”

Sell the one operational truth a fabrication business needs:

> One surveyed/design record should drive the GHS quotation, material and hardware schedule, cutting plan, factory work order, quality checks, dispatch and client update — without staff re-entering the same job in separate tools.

This is compelling because these companies sell made-to-measure work. A measurement, profile, glass-hole, hardware or price error becomes scrap, delay, margin loss and an unhappy architect/client. The first product is therefore not a broad ERP. It is a **quote-to-factory pack and job-control system** for Ghanaian glass, aluminium and façade contractors.

SOFAAMY already proves that Veloxa understands the hard domain: separate models for framed aluminium, frameless glass and curtain wall; GHS estimates; cut/BOM outputs; production/QA/dispatch workflow; project-level quoting; and WhatsApp-facing client communication. See [PROJECT_OUTLINE.md](PROJECT_OUTLINE.md) and [INDUSTRY-REPORT.md](INDUSTRY-REPORT.md).

## 2. Ideal customer profile and disqualifiers

### The five customers we want first

Prioritise contractors that meet at least four of these:

- Fabricate and install aluminium windows/doors, frameless glass, curtain wall or façades—not just sell materials.
- Handle commercial, institutional, estate, multi-site or repeat projects.
- Have a workshop/factory, a field-installation team, or multiple branches.
- Quote custom jobs and coordinate several handoffs: sales, survey, design, procurement, factory, installation and accounts.
- Have a founder, MD, operations or technical lead accessible through a legitimate company channel.
- Will run a real job through a paid pilot and provide sample price lists/workflow rules.

### Do not force a sale

Disqualify or nurture when the company:

- only sells profiles/glass and does not run fabrication jobs;
- completes mostly one-off residential work with no recurring job volume;
- wants free bespoke development before paying for discovery;
- cannot nominate an operational owner or share a non-sensitive sample job;
- needs every workflow customised before proving the core quote → factory-pack flow.

That discipline protects the platform from becoming “Sofaamy copy number two.”

## 4. The offer: a productised landing path

Do not start with a six-month “custom build” quote. Build commitment in three steps.

| Stage | What customer receives | What Veloxa learns | Commercial rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Paid workflow discovery | Two workshops, job-shadow/map, gap scorecard, configured pilot brief and success measures. | Product mix, approvals, data quality, buyer urgency. | Fixed fee credited against setup if they proceed. Never free consulting. |
| 2. Paid live-job pilot | One product family and one real job through quote, BOM/factory pack and selected job stages. | Adoption behaviour, data mappings, ROI proof and implementation risks. | 30 days; clear owner; weekly check-in; no unlimited scope. |
| 3. Subscription rollout | Configured workspace, onboarding, roles, reporting and support. | Retention / expansion signals. | Setup fee + monthly subscription; add modules only after core usage is stable. |

### Recommended commercial packaging (validate with five discovery calls)

| Package | Best for | Scope boundary | Indicative commercial test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core Fabrication | Small-to-mid fabricator | CRM/intake, GHS quote, design/BOQ/factory pack, pipeline, basic users. | GHS 2,500–4,500/month + GHS 12,000–25,000 setup. |
| Operations | Multi-team fabricator | Core plus cutting optimisation, inventory/procurement, QA, dispatch, management dashboard. | GHS 6,000–10,000/month + GHS 35,000–75,000 setup. |
| Enterprise Façade | Complex/regional contractor | Operations plus integrations, multi-site controls, migration and implementation support. | Custom, anchored to verified value and rollout scope. |

These are **hypotheses, not published prices**. Keep Sofaamy’s bespoke deal separate. Price the subscription on recurring operational value, support cost and deployment complexity—not the historic project build cost.

## 6. First 30 business days

### Week 1: prepare an honest sellable pilot

1. Establish **Veloxa branding, terms, privacy/data-processing position and support SLA** independent of Sofaamy.
2. Create a 12-minute demo with three scenes: frame quote → factory pack; frameless glass order → hardware list; project pipeline → QA/dispatch.
3. Prepare a one-page pilot brief and a discovery scorecard. The scorecard must ask for volume, product mix, current tools, price-list readiness, job handoffs, named owner, budget range and start date.
4. Put a clear “not a generic CRM; not an unlimited custom build” boundary in every proposal.

### Weeks 2–3: account-based outreach

1. Work one company at a time, starting with the five priority accounts. Personalise with a public, verifiable trigger: project type, workshop scale, multi-site work or named service.
2. Start with a short email to a named decision maker or the company’s official inbox. For public business phone numbers, ask permission before sending WhatsApp material.
3. The objective is **a 20-minute discovery**, not a demo. Ask: “How does a measured job become a quote, factory instruction and delivered installation today?”
4. Within 24 hours, send a one-page recap naming the stated cost of delay/rework and the proposed pilot boundary.
5. Follow up once after five business days; then nurture. No spam sequence.

### Week 4: convert two discoveries to a paid pilot

1. Score every discovery against urgency, data readiness, decision access, product fit, job volume, budget and time-to-start.
2. Offer only the most ready two a paid pilot. Each has one executive sponsor, one operational owner, one real job and a weekly success review.
3. Close a rollout only after the pilot proves a defined before/after outcome.

## 7. Pilot success measures that create a case study

Agree baselines before configuration. Track only what the customer can verify.

- Quote turnaround time from received survey to client-ready quotation.
- Number of manual re-entries from design/measurement into another tool.
- Number and cost of quote/drawing/material-pack corrections for the pilot job.
- Time from approved quote to factory-ready pack.
- Stage-update completeness and time to identify blocked jobs.
- Adoption: weekly active users in the roles agreed for the pilot.

The case study is the asset that unlocks Ghana: “A real contractor ran a real job with X fewer handoffs and Y faster quote-to-factory release.” Do not promise a percentage before measuring it.

## 8. Why this can work

1. **It solves an expensive, repeated operational failure.** In made-to-measure fabrication, re-entry and ungoverned changes create very visible cost: wrong glass, wrong cuts, rework, late sites and lost margin.
2. **The product has a sharper wedge than generic business software.** The domain outputs—glass order drawings, hardware schedules, profiles/cutting optimisation, GHS quoting and factory stages—are difficult to reproduce with a general CRM or accounting app.
3. **Ghana-local operations matter.** Local GHS pricing, payment gates, WhatsApp-heavy communication and hands-on onboarding reduce adoption friction versus a distant generic tool.
4. **The buyer market is identifiable.** The researched targets publicly advertise exactly the product families and project complexity matched by the platform.
5. **The reference can be powerful without owning the code.** Sofaamy can be a permissioned reference/pilot case study. Veloxa retains its reusable platform and learns what becomes configuration versus a core feature.

## 9. Why it might fail—and the solution

| Failure mode | Why it is real | Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| “Every fabricator is different.” | True workflows and profile rules differ. Unlimited tailoring destroys SaaS margin. | Define a configuration layer: catalogue, roles, stages, templates, price rules and document branding. Charge separately for non-repeatable bespoke engineering. |
| The product is almost done but not reliable enough for paid jobs. | Fabricators will not forgive incorrect quotes or factory documents. | Use a narrow pilot and shadow/parallel run. Release only validated product families. Preserve human approval gates and audit trails. |
| Customers expect software to be free or one-off. | Many SMEs buy tools only after a painful incident. | Sell a paid discovery and ROI-backed pilot, not a feature list. Target companies with workshop/project scale first. |
| No clear local support or implementation team. | Adoption fails when staff are trained once and abandoned. | Name an implementation owner, deliver role-based training, WhatsApp/phone support hours, onboarding checklist and weekly pilot review. |
| Wrong buyer: sales loves it, factory ignores it. | Cross-functional software dies without operational ownership. | Require MD sponsor plus factory/operations owner before pilot. Make factory-pack success the first value proof. |
| Sofaamy relationship creates ownership confusion. | Prospects may think this is Sofaamy’s private tool. | Put platform IP, data ownership, branding and case-study permission in writing. Say: “Veloxa platform, configured for your company; your data remains yours.” |
| Sales cycle is longer than the cash runway. | Tier-1 contractors may have enterprise procurement habits. | Run a barbell: 2 enterprise accounts + 3 founder-led mid-market accounts. Sell fixed-scope pilots, collect setup fees, and keep a weekly pipeline cadence. |
| Data migration becomes the entire project. | Old Excel/WhatsApp information is messy. | Start with new live jobs. Import only minimum client/catalogue data. Price migration separately after the core workflow is adopted. |

## 10. Non-negotiable product and commercial work before scaling

1. Multi-tenant isolation: each customer’s users, data, catalogues and files must be isolated and exportable.
2. A configurable tenant layer, not code forks: branding, document templates, stages, roles, rate cards, product rules and integrations.
3. Subscription administration: tenant provisioning, billing, support entitlement, backup/restore, audit logs and offboarding/export path.
4. Production reliability: database backups, secure file storage, monitoring, role permissions, document-version control and approval gates.
5. A clear implementation playbook: discovery inputs, catalogue onboarding, test-job acceptance, training, parallel run, go-live and support.
6. Customer contracts covering platform IP, customer-data ownership, support, service limits, payment, change requests and case-study consent.

## 11. The blunt answer

This can become a serious Ghanaian vertical SaaS business **if** Veloxa treats it as a repeatable operating platform, not a bespoke development studio wearing a SaaS label. The first five customers should be chosen for workflow similarity, willingness to pay for a constrained pilot, and ability to become references—not merely because they are large names.

It will fail if we ship unvalidated factory outputs, accept every custom request into the core product, or depend on a single Sofaamy relationship. The answer is a narrow paid pilot, strict configuration-versus-custom boundaries, proven implementation, and five referenceable Ghanaian accounts before Africa-wide expansion.
